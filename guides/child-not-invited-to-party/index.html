What to Do When Your Child Isn't Invited to a Party

By Arianna Andersen, Ph.D. · Updated 2026-11-10

Finding out that classmates went to a party without them can hurt a child deeply. It can hurt parents too. The first instinct is often to fix it right away by calling another parent or promising a bigger party. A calmer first step usually helps more.

Start by listening

Let your child tell you what happened in their own words. Name the feeling without rushing past it: "You felt left out, and that is really hard." Avoid jumping straight to "They were not real friends anyway." Your child may still like those children and want to be included.

Separate the facts from the story

Children often decide that being left out means nobody likes them. Gently help them sort what they know from what they are guessing.

  • Maybe the party had a small guest limit.
  • Maybe the families know each other outside school.
  • Maybe the other child chose friends from a different activity.
  • Maybe there is a friendship habit worth practicing.

Not every invitation is personal. Not every friendship will go both ways, either. Both can be true.

Look for friends who choose you back

One of the most useful lessons is noticing who is already kind and interested. Ask your child: "Who sat with you this week? Who laughs at your jokes? Who asked you to play?" Those children are good candidates for a playdate. Putting effort into friendships that go both ways is a healthier goal than chasing one child who keeps saying no.

Check for a habit to practice

Sometimes a pattern pushes peers away. A child might take over games, ignore turns in conversation, or insist on their own way. If you notice something like that, pick one specific habit and practice it at home in a low-pressure way. Keep the tone encouraging. The message is "Here is a skill we can practice," not "This is why you were left out."

What to avoid

  • Do not contact the host family to complain about the invitation list.
  • Do not promise your child that everyone will be friends.
  • Do not post about the party or talk about it in front of other families.

Common questions

Should I call the other parent?

Usually not about the invitation itself. If there is a pattern of exclusion or unkind behavior at school, talk with the teacher instead.

How can I help my child make new friends?

Invite one child who already shows interest for a short, structured playdate with a planned activity.
