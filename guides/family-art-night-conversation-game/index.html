A Family Art Night Game That Builds Conversation Skills

By Arianna Andersen, Ph.D. · Updated 2026-11-10

Looking at art together is an easy way to practice conversation skills without anyone feeling put on the spot. There is no wrong answer about a painting. That makes it a safe place to practice listening, taking turns, and respecting different opinions.

How to play

  1. Choose one painting. A book, a museum website, or a family art display on your TV all work.
  2. Each person says one thing they notice.
  3. The next person gives a related response before adding their own idea: "I noticed the boat too. I also see a bird."
  4. Ask one question to the next person: "What do you think the person is feeling?"
  5. Take turns until everyone has had at least two turns.

Skills it practices

  • Return the turn. Respond to what someone said before switching topics.
  • Don't yuck someone's yum. If you do not like the painting, say so kindly and ask what they like about it.
  • Listen to the whole answer. Wait until the speaker is completely finished.

Good paintings to start with

Pick works with lots to notice: busy scenes, animals, weather, or people doing everyday things. Landscapes by Bierstadt, harbor scenes by Monet, and everyday interiors by Vermeer all give children plenty to talk about.

Common questions

What age is this for?

Preschoolers can name what they see. Older children can talk about feelings, stories, and opinions.

Do we need a museum?

No. Any picture book, printed image, or art shown on a TV works.
