How to Help a Child Join a Game at Recess

By Arianna Andersen, Ph.D. · Updated 2026-11-10

Joining a game that is already happening is one of the hardest social moves for many children. Some hang back and never ask. Others run straight in and change the rules. Both children want to play. They just need a clearer plan.

The four-step plan

  1. Watch. Spend a minute noticing what the game is and how it is played.
  2. Wait for an opening. Look for a natural break, like the end of a round.
  3. Ask. "Can I play?" or "Can I be on a team next round?"
  4. Follow their rules. If invited, play the game the way the group is already playing it.

Why watching matters

Watching first tells a child what the game is, who is leading it, and when a good moment to ask might come. It also shows the group that the child is interested in their game, not trying to take it over.

What if they say no?

Rehearse this part too. A calm response keeps the door open: "Okay, maybe next time." Then your child can look for another group or a different activity. Pushing, arguing, or joining anyway usually makes the next "no" more likely.

Practice at home

  • Start a simple game with a sibling or parent and have your child practice the four steps to join.
  • Role-play both a "yes" and a "no" so neither answer is a surprise.
  • Talk about which groups at school seem welcoming and which games your child already knows.

Help from school

If your child keeps getting stuck, ask the teacher or recess staff which games are popular and whether there are structured activities your child could join. Knowing the rules in advance makes asking much easier.

Common questions

What should my child say to join?

A short, friendly question works best: "Can I play?" or "Can I join next round?"

My child joins but then takes over. What helps?

Practice the fourth step: following the group's rules. Praise specific moments when your child goes along with someone else's idea.
