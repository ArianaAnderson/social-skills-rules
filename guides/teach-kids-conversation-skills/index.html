How to Teach Kids Conversation Skills: Return the Turn

By Arianna Andersen, Ph.D. · Updated 2026-11-10

Some children love to talk but have trouble with the back-and-forth part of conversation. They may launch into a favorite topic, answer a question with one word, or switch subjects before a friend has finished. Other children want to talk but freeze because they do not know what to say next. In both cases the missing piece is the same. A conversation is an exchange, and each person needs a turn.

The two-part formula: related response plus quick question

Give your child one pattern they can remember. When a friend says something, your child gives a related response and then asks a quick question. That is it.

  • Friend: "I went to the lake this weekend."
  • Related response: "That sounds fun. I like swimming."
  • Quick question: "Did you go in the water?"

The related response shows that your child heard the friend. The question hands the turn back. Then your child waits and listens to the answer before adding anything new.

Why "just ask questions" is not enough

A child who only asks questions can sound like an interviewer. A child who only shares can sound like they are giving a speech. The formula keeps both children in the conversation. It also gives a child a way to stay on the friend's topic for at least one turn, which is often the hardest step.

A 5-minute practice game at home

  1. Sit across from your child and say one simple sentence about your day.
  2. Your child gives a related response and one quick question.
  3. Answer the question, then switch roles.
  4. Play three to five rounds. Stop while it is still fun.
  5. Point out one specific thing they did well, such as "You asked about my lunch, not about your game. That kept us talking."

Keep the topics light at first: food, pets, weekend plans, a show you both watched. Once the pattern feels easy, try topics your child finds less interesting. That is where the skill really gets practiced.

Common sticking points

  • Topic jumping. If your child switches to their own interest, pause and ask, "What did I just tell you about?" Then let them try the related response again.
  • One-word answers. Teach "answer plus one more thing." For example: "Yes. We had pizza."
  • Interrupting. Practice waiting until the other person's sentence is completely finished. A silent count of one or two helps some children.

Adapt it to your child

Some children communicate with fewer words, use a device, or need extra processing time. The goal is not a perfect script. The goal is noticing the other person and giving them a turn. Adjust pace, language, and expectations so the practice feels doable.

Common questions

What age can children start practicing conversation turns?

Many children can practice a simple related response and question by preschool or early elementary age with adult modeling. Adjust the length and topics to the child.

How long should a practice session last?

Five minutes is plenty. Short, frequent practice usually works better than one long session.
